Work sample: Your First 15 Minutes

A five-minute mobile microlearning for pantry and partner agency volunteer leads. It teaches one thing well: how to welcome a new volunteer so they come back. Tap through it below. It is built from the onboarding approach I used at Green City Market, rewritten for a pantry setting.

Works on a phone with no app or sign-in. Built by hand in HTML to show the design; in production I would build it in the team's authoring tool and LMS.

Design notes

Learners
Volunteer leads at pantries and partner agencies. Many are volunteers themselves. They learn between tasks, mostly on a phone, and have no time for a long module.
The problem
New volunteers who spend their first shift standing around are less likely to come back. The fix is not more information. It is a better first fifteen minutes.
Learning objectives
After this lesson, a volunteer lead can:
  1. Greet a new volunteer and give them one clear job and a buddy within five minutes of arrival.
  2. Split a large walk-in group into small crews led by returning volunteers.
  3. Deliver a two-minute safety talk covering lifting, gloves and the first aid kit.
  4. Close the shift by asking each new volunteer to sign up for the next one.
Approach
Microlearning (eight cards, about five minutes), two decisions drawn from real shift situations with feedback on every choice, and a checklist job aid the lead can keep open during the shift.
Why it is built this way
The checklist does the remembering so the lesson does not have to. The scenarios practice the judgment calls that go wrong in real life, like a corporate group arriving early with no shift lead.

How I would measure it

LevelMeasure
ReactionCompletion rate and a one-tap "was this useful?" on the last card
LearningFirst-try accuracy on the two decision cards
BehaviorSpot check: do leads use the checklist before doors open?
ResultsShare of first-time volunteers who return within 60 days, compared with the same period before launch

This is the evaluation plan, not results. The sample has not been piloted yet.
