The onboarding piece: Your First 15 Minutes

A new volunteer usually decides on the first shift whether to come back. The person who controls that moment is the shift lead, who's slammed. So I built the training for the lead.

What the lead practices, in 3 minutes

  1. Welcome a first-timer with their name, one job and a buddy.
  2. Split a big group into small crews led by returning volunteers.
  3. Give a two-minute safety talk before the first box moves.
  4. Close the shift by booking the next one, on the spot.
Play it (3 minutes, works on a phone)
Where it sits in a program It's step 3 of the cycle above. A QR code on the sign-in table, no login. After play, leads keep a one-page job aid. To measure it: first-timers back within 60 days, compared with before. It hasn't been piloted yet; that's the plan.

What I'd do in my first 60 days

For a national volunteer corps run through regional managers. A plan, not a promise of results.

For the National MS Society: this is how I'd start with the MS Ambassador program and the regional volunteer engagement managers.

Days 1–20 · Listen
  • One-on-ones with each regional manager: what's working, what's stuck.
  • Sit in on a volunteer training and talk with a few volunteers in each region.
  • Pull a year of data from the volunteer platform: sign-up to first activity, and where people drop off.
Days 21–40 · Map
  • Walk the intake path as a new volunteer and mark every step that slows people down.
  • Agree with the managers on a short scorecard: time to first activity, 90-day retention, satisfaction.
Days 41–60 · Fix one thing
  • Pilot one intake fix in one region and compare it with the others.
  • Start a monthly story pipeline: each manager sends one volunteer story for mission communications.
  • Set a monthly managers' huddle around the scorecard.
