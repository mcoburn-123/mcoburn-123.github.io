Two projects, one method

One is fieldwork across a region. The other is a city's plan, from the inside. Both started the same way: listen first, map who's there, then decide.

Observe · regional food recovery study

Who already moves food, and who's tossing it?

  • Managed the outreach for a study covering 28 communities and 49 ZIP codes.
  • Reached 750 businesses. 250+ answered, and 41 completed the survey.
  • Built the findings deck and the partner map, later shown in a public webinar.
  • Sent weekly status reports to the project director and tracked hours against budget.
Involve · a city's clean jobs plan

What would residents actually want built here?

  • Research lead on a graduate client project for a California city.
  • Ran the meetings with city staff and interviewed local stakeholders.
  • Wrote the clean tech sectors analysis and the housing and zoning lens.
  • Our pitch back to the city was one plain idea: clean jobs at home.

What carries over to any neighborhood: a partner map before a plan, outreach people can actually answer, and a report back so they can see what their answers changed.

Where I've done it

The five rungs come from the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation, a standard many cities and agencies use. The promises are paraphrased; the examples are mine.

Pick the rung

Three situations I'd expect in the first month. Make your call, then see mine. There's no score. Reasonable people pick differently, and the point is to pick on purpose.

My first 90 days

The same order a grower uses on new ground: read the site, plant small, then tend what took.

Days 1–30 · observe
  • Listening tour: partners, block clubs, schools, elected offices, front-line staff.
  • Map the field: who's anchoring, who's active, who we haven't met.
  • Write down every promise already made to the community, kept or not.
Days 31–60 · plant
  • Pick the rung for each project and say it out loud.
  • Set a meeting rhythm and one shared tracker partners can see.
  • Two small wins with partners who said yes early.
Days 61–90 · tend
  • Report back: what we heard, what changed because of it, what didn't and why.
  • Close the loops on every open ask from the listening tour.
  • A one-page scorecard leadership and partners both read.

A plan to start from. It changes once I've heard from the people it's for, which is the point of days 1–30.
